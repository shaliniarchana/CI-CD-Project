Funny Real-World Example: “Pizza Hotel Counter”

Webpack = Pizza Chef 🍳
You have separate ingredients: dough (HTML), sauce (CSS), and toppings (JS).
Webpack mixes everything together and puts it in one neat pizza box (dist/bundle.js) so it’s ready to eat.
Without Webpack, your toppings are scattered on the counter — messy!

ESLint = Health Inspector 👮‍♂️
Checks your kitchen (JS code) for mistakes:
Is there an extra onion nobody ordered? (unused variable)
Did you spill sauce on the floor? (syntax error)
Keeps your code “clean and hygienic” before it goes out.

dist = The Pizza Box 📦
After the chef (Webpack) finishes, everything is neatly packed in the pizza box (dist/).
This is the ready-to-serve version of your app.
Customers (users) never see your messy kitchen (src/), only the finished product.

CI/CD = The Delivery Robot 🤖
Every time you make a new pizza (push code to GitHub), the robot:
Checks the kitchen is clean (runs ESLint)
Lets the chef cook (Webpack build)
Puts the pizza in a box (dist folder)
Delivers it automatically to your customer (GitHub Pages)
You don’t have to run around delivering pizzas yourself!

Docker = The Magic Lunchbox 🥡
Imagine your customer has a completely different kitchen. The pizza might not cook the same way there.
Docker packs your pizza, chef, ingredients, and oven into a self-contained lunchbox (container).
Your friend (or server) can open the lunchbox anywhere, and it works exactly the same.
Keeps everything isolated — no messy kitchen, no missing toppings, no broken oven!
